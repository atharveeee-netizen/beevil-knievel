## 🔬 Seminal Bio-Acoustic Studies

### 1. Monitoring of Swarming Sounds in Bee Hives for Early Detection
* **Authors**: Sara Ferrari, Marcella Silva, Marcella Guarino, Daniel Berckmans
* **Journal**: *Computers and Electronics in Agriculture*, Vol. 64, Issue 1, pp. 72-77 (2008)
* **DOI / URL**: [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2008.05.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2008.05.010)
* **Key Findings**:
  - Continuous sound recording inside the beehive reveals significant acoustic frequency shifts in the **200 Hz to 450 Hz** frequency band prior to swarming.
  - Approximately 24 to 48 hours before swarm departure, high-amplitude acoustic peaks emerge around **400 Hz to 500 Hz**, generated by virgin queen piping and worker agitation.
  - Acoustic power spectral density (PSD) provides reliable predictive lead-time compared to visual inspection.
* **Relevance to BEEVIL KNIEVEL**: Grounds the parameterization of BEEVIL's CMSIS-DSP FFT bins (Band 4: 350-500 Hz) for automated pre-swarm alarm generation.
* **Access Date**: September 2026

### 2. Identification of the Honey Bee Swarming Process Through Continuous Vibration Monitoring
* **Authors**: Martin Bencsik, Jeremy Bencsik, Matthew Baxter, et al.
* **Journal**: *PLOS ONE*, Vol. 6, Issue 2, e16741 (2011)
* **DOI / URL**: [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0016741](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0016741)
* **Key Findings**:
  - Continuous automated acoustic and vibration sensing identifies long-term acoustic patterns preceding reproductive swarming.
  - Demonstrates that worker bee wing-fanning displays a fundamental frequency peak between **180 Hz and 240 Hz** during normal hive homeostasis, which abruptly broadens and increases in harmonic intensity prior to colony departure.
* **Relevance to BEEVIL KNIEVEL**: Validates the 180-240 Hz frequency band as the baseline healthy colony homeostatic marker in `bee_acoustic_classifier.py`.
* **Access Date**: September 2026

### 3. A Preliminary Study of Sounds Emitted by Honey Bees in a Beehive (NU-Hive Project)
* **Authors**: Stefania Cecchi, Alessandro Terenzi, Simone Orcioni, Paola Riolo, Sara Ruschioni, Nunzio Isidoro
* **Conference**: *Audio Engineering Society (AES) Convention 144*, Milan, Italy (2018)
* **Paper / URL**: [http://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=19515](http://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=19515)
* **Key Findings**:
  - Evaluates dual-microphone recording setups inside Langstroth beehives across controlled queenright and queenless conditions.
  - Establishes that queenless colonies exhibit a characteristic "queenless roar" or distress hum, marked by sustained acoustic power shifts into the **285 Hz to 350 Hz** spectral band.
  - Proves that external traffic, bird songs, and wind noise occupy frequency bands above 800 Hz and below 100 Hz, allowing digital bandpass filtering to suppress environmental false alarms.
* **Relevance to BEEVIL KNIEVEL**: Primary experimental foundation for BEEVIL's 4-band spectral feature extractor and environmental noise suppression filter.
* **Access Date**: September 2026

## 📊 Public Audio Datasets & Benchmark Standards

### 4. "To Bee or Not to Bee: An Annotated Dataset for Beehive Sound Recognition" (Zenodo Record 1321278)
* **Principal Investigators**: Inês Nolasco, Emmanouil Benetos (Queen Mary University of London)
* **Repository**: Zenodo Open Research Repository
* **DOI / URL**: [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1321278](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1321278)
* **License**: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
* **Dataset Characteristics**:
  - 78 high-resolution field recordings across 8 hives in Europe, North America, and Australia.
  - Total duration: ~12 hours of audio annotated into `Bee` and `noBee` segments.
  - Direct comparative audio files: `Hive1_12_06_2018_QueenBee_H1` vs `Hive1_31_05_2018_NO_QueenBee_H1`.
* **Relevance to BEEVIL KNIEVEL**: Official external benchmark dataset evaluated by BEEVIL's `TinyML Model/bee_acoustic_classifier.py` and `run_level1_testing.py`.
* **Access Date**: September 2026
